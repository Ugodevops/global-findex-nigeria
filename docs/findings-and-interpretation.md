# Findings and Interpretation

## Reporting note

This document records the figures retained from the completed Power BI visuals and project analysis. Weighted and unweighted results are identified separately. Percentage-point differences are calculated by subtracting the lower percentage from the higher percentage.

The findings are descriptive. They do not prove causation.

## 1. Digital access: internet use by mobile phone ownership

**Measure:** Unweighted percentages.

| Mobile phone ownership category | Does not use internet | Uses internet |
|---|---:|---:|
| Owns a mobile phone | 41.09% | 58.91% |
| Does not own a mobile phone | 87.50% | 12.50% |

**Observation:** Internet use is 46.41 percentage points higher among mobile phone owners than among respondents without a mobile phone.

**Interpretation:** The comparison shows an association between mobile phone ownership and internet use in the analysed records. It does not show that phone ownership alone causes internet use.

**Reservation:** These percentages are unweighted and should not be presented as weighted national estimates or directly compared with the weighted results below.

## 2. Internet-use category and financial inclusion

**Measure:** Weighted percentages as reported in the Power BI visuals.

| Financial inclusion indicator | Internet-use category | No/DK/Refused category |
|---|---:|---:|
| Digital payment participation | 83.42% | 36.94% |
| Financial institution account ownership | 86.17% | 43.38% |

| Indicator | Difference |
|---|---:|
| Digital payment participation | 46.48 percentage points |
| Financial institution account ownership | 42.79 percentage points |

**Observation:** The internet-use category records higher percentages for both reported financial inclusion indicators.

**Interpretation:** The results suggest an association between the reported internet-use classification and financial institution account ownership and digital payment participation.

**Reservations:**
- The No/DK/Refused category must retain its original label. It should not be described as a group made up exclusively of confirmed non-internet users.
- A verified mobile money account ownership percentage for this particular comparison was not retained in the final project record. It is intentionally omitted rather than estimated or invented.
- The results do not establish that internet use causes financial inclusion.

## 3. Age and financial inclusion

**Measure:** Weighted percentages.

| Age group | Financial institution account ownership | Digital payment participation | Mobile money account ownership |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 15–20 | 31.70% | 30.18% | 19.84% |
| 21–30 | 68.81% | 65.86% | 43.22% |
| 31–40 | 75.41% | 65.68% | 34.52% |
| 41–50 | 66.09% | 63.16% | 35.58% |
| Above 50 | 59.87% | 46.05% | 27.38% |

**Observations:**

- The 31–40 age group records the highest financial institution account ownership (75.41%).
- The 21–30 age group records the highest digital payment participation (65.86%) and mobile money account ownership (43.22%).
- The 15–20 age group records the lowest percentage across all three indicators.
- The above-50 age group records lower digital payment participation and mobile money ownership than the 21–30 and 31–40 groups.

**Interpretation:** Financial inclusion patterns vary by age. Account ownership and participation in digital financial activities do not necessarily peak in the same age group.

**Reservation:** The available age visual does not provide an age-by-internet-use comparison. The findings therefore describe age differences in financial inclusion only; they do not establish how internet use interacts with age.

## 4. Gender and financial inclusion

**Measure:** Weighted percentages.

| Financial inclusion indicator | Male | Female | Male–female gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Financial institution account ownership | 70.11% | 49.34% | 20.77 percentage points |
| Digital payment participation | 63.08% | 45.87% | 17.21 percentage points |
| Mobile money account ownership | 40.56% | 25.02% | 15.54 percentage points |

**Observations:**

- Male respondents record higher percentages than female respondents across all three indicators.
- The largest reported gap is in financial institution account ownership (20.77 percentage points).
- The digital payment participation gap is 17.21 percentage points.
- The mobile money account ownership gap is 15.54 percentage points.

**Interpretation:** The reported financial inclusion indicators show gender differences in the analysed data.

**Reservation:** The available visual does not provide direct male/female comparisons for mobile phone ownership or internet use. No conclusion is drawn about gender differences in those digital technology indicators. The observed differences do not explain their causes.

## 5. Geographical differences: urban and rural

**Measure:** Weighted percentages.

| Financial inclusion indicator | Urban | Rural | Urban–rural gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Financial institution account ownership | 68.32% | 52.03% | 16.29 percentage points |
| Digital payment participation | 59.03% | 50.40% | 8.63 percentage points |
| Mobile money account ownership | 37.58% | 28.49% | 9.09 percentage points |

**Observations:**

- Urban respondents record higher percentages than rural respondents across all three indicators.
- The largest reported difference is in financial institution account ownership (16.29 percentage points).
- The mobile money account ownership gap is 9.09 percentage points.
- The digital payment participation gap is 8.63 percentage points.

**Interpretation:** The results show geographical differences in the financial inclusion indicators examined.

**Reservation:** The available visual does not provide direct rural/urban comparisons for mobile phone ownership or internet use. No conclusions are drawn about geographical differences in those digital technology indicators. The observed differences do not establish their causes.

## 6. Overall synthesis

The available analysis shows several descriptive patterns:

1. Internet use is more common among mobile phone owners than among respondents without a mobile phone in the unweighted comparison.
2. The internet-use category records higher weighted percentages for financial institution account ownership and digital payment participation than the No/DK/Refused category.
3. Financial inclusion varies across age groups, with different age groups recording the highest percentages for different indicators.
4. Male respondents record higher percentages than female respondents across the three financial inclusion indicators.
5. Urban respondents record higher percentages than rural respondents across the three financial inclusion indicators.

These results support further investigation of digital access and financial inclusion in Nigeria. They do not establish that digital technology, age, gender or residence independently causes the observed differences.

## 7. Cross-cutting limitations

- The mobile phone/internet comparison is unweighted, while the other comparisons above are weighted. They are not directly interchangeable.
- The No/DK/Refused category is not equivalent to a confirmed No response.
- The age-by-internet-use comparison is not available in the retained visual results.
- Direct gender comparisons for mobile phone ownership and internet use are not available.
- Direct rural/urban comparisons for mobile phone ownership and internet use are not available.
- The mobile-money result for the internet-use comparison is omitted because a verified figure was not retained.
- The official questionnaire and data dictionary should be consulted to confirm exact variable universes, response coding, sample design and weighting before reproducing or making population-level claims.

## 8. Conclusion

The Global Findex Nigeria project demonstrates how survey microdata can be used to investigate the relationship between digital technology and financial inclusion. The completed visuals reveal differences across internet-use categories, age groups, gender and residential location.

The results are useful as descriptive evidence, provided that weighting, categories and missing comparisons are made explicit. The analysis should be extended only when the relevant source variables, definitions and denominators have been verified.
